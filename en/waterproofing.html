Waterproofing Materials

Waterproofing materials

Seven series, from substrate sealing and the main membrane through to topcoat protection and full PU systems — specified to the substrate and location.

How to choose. A complete waterproofing build-up is normally three layers: primer → membrane → topcoat. The primer seals the substrate and strengthens adhesion; the membrane — compound or flexible cement depending on location — does the waterproofing; the topcoat resists UV and staining. Existing leaks and cracks should be treated first with the plugging and crystalline range.
FAQ

Frequently asked questions

How do I choose a roof waterproofing material?

Confirm four things first: substrate type and condition, drainage, whether the roof takes foot traffic, and the number of workable days available. For a standard flat concrete roof the build-up is primer, compound membrane and topcoat. Roofs that pond water or take traffic are better served by a solvent-based PU system. Metal roofs are primarily a thermal problem — treat fasteners and overlaps first, then coat.

What is the difference between flexible cement and PU?

Flexible cement is a two-part resin and aggregate system with the best compatibility with concrete, suited to internal wet areas and free of odour. Solvent-based PU is a two-part polyurethane with the highest film strength, elongation and water resistance, suited to ponding, trafficked or abraded areas, but it costs more and requires accurate mixing.

The roof leaked again within two years. Is that a material fault?

Usually not. The most common causes, in order, are: applying over a substrate that is still too wet; failing to remove chalking and failed coatings; coating over cracks without reinforcement; poor detailing at parapets and outlets; and insufficient coverage or inadequate recoat intervals.

How do I calculate how much material is needed?

Area × coverage rate × number of coats. For example, 100 m² at 0.6 kg/m² over two coats requires around 120 kg. Rough substrates increase consumption, so allow an additional 10–15% when estimating. If site consumption is well below the calculated figure, the film is too thin.

Can material be applied to a damp surface?

Most water-based products require moisture content below 10%. Crystalline penetrating and plugging materials are the exception — they rely on moisture to react with the concrete and form sealing crystals, so damp substrates are acceptable and often expected.
