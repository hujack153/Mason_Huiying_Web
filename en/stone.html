Stone-Effect Wall Coating

Why Stone-Effect

Why use coating instead of stone

Granite has long been used for interior and exterior cladding for its distinctive veining. But quarrying causes permanent environmental damage, and weight, transport and installation costs are all high. A stone-effect coating reproduces the appearance and texture while resolving the weight and sustainability problem.

Over 90% visual match

A single product carries five or more colour particles that merge on spraying, producing natural colour and fine texture that reads as stone at any distance.

Dense, hard film

Good water and alkali resistance, excellent resistance to corrosion, high crack resistance and durability — long service life without noticeable fading.

Water resistant and dense

Particle size and colour consistency are tightly controlled to reproduce the natural veining and tactile quality of high-grade stone in an environmentally responsible product.

Coating System

Build-up and application sequence

Seven layers from substrate to protective coat: substrate → levelling → penetrating sealer primer → pigmented base coat → stone-effect coating → clear topcoat → joint coating.

Application requirements: compressor line diameter must be at least equal to the spray line diameter; spray line 8 mm × 12 mm; delivery hose from pot to gun under 4 m; compressor 7.5 KW or above with working pressure of 8 kg minimum (11–12 kg recommended); pressure pot regulated to 0.6–0.8 kg and never above 2.5 kg; pot, lines and gun to be cleaned at the end of each day; during short breaks the gun should be immersed in water to prevent material drying and blocking the nozzle.

Equipment: roller, spray gun, pressure pot, compressor.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

How does stone-effect coating compare with real granite?

Visually it reaches over 90% match, reproducing the veining and tactile quality of natural stone at both distance and close range. The main differences are weight and cost: a coating needs no cladding support structure, which substantially reduces structural load and installation cost, and avoids the environmental impact of quarrying. Durability depends on the full system being installed correctly.

What layers make up the complete system?

Seven layers: substrate, levelling, penetrating sealer primer, pigmented base coat, stone-effect coating, clear topcoat and joint coating. The joint coating divides the elevation and is key to the realism of the finish. Each layer has a distinct function; omitting any one affects both appearance and durability.

What equipment is required?

The stone-effect coating must be applied with a dedicated spray gun. Equipment includes a compressor (7.5 KW or above, 8 kg minimum working pressure, 11–12 kg recommended), a pressure pot (regulated to 0.6–0.8 kg, never above 2.5 kg), spray line of 8 mm × 12 mm with a delivery hose under 4 m, and rollers. Primer and base coat can be roller or spray applied.

How long does the full sequence take?

It is governed by recoat intervals: 8 hours after levelling, 4 hours after primer, 8 hours after base coat, and 24 hours after the stone-effect coating before the protective coat. In normal weather a single elevation takes around four to six working days from preparation to completion, subject to temperature and humidity.

Can colours be specified?

Yes. A single panel can carry white, black, grey, orange and rust particles simultaneously, and colours can be adjusted to the design. We recommend producing a site sample panel for client and supervisor approval before proceeding, which then serves as the reference for handover inspection.

Need a sample panel?

We can produce panels to a specified colour for client and supervisor approval, to serve as the handover reference.
